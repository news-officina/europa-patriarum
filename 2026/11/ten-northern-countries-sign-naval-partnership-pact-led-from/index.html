Ten northern countries sign naval partnership pact led from Britain's Northwood

Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom sign the agreement, which keeps the forces under national authority. The United States is not part of it.

9 November 2026

Ten Joint Expeditionary Force countries sign an agreement today partnering their naval forces to deter Russian aggression in the region. The signatories are Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom. Norway, according to its government, proposed the initiative for a broad maritime partnership.

Under the pact, the joint naval forces will be commanded from Northwood, the British maritime headquarters outside London, while remaining under the authority of the participating governments. General Gwyn Jenkins, Britain's First Sea Lord, said the grouping would complement NATO and respond to crises faster. Norway framed it, in the words of Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, as a direct response to American calls for Europe to assume more responsibility for Allied security. Støre said Europe and Canada were making a significant contribution to security in the North Atlantic and High North.

In July, according to a Royal Navy report, the ten had agreed under Britain's Maritime Proposition to align doctrine, training, planning, sustainment and capability development so that equipment, parts, ammunition or personnel of one navy could be substituted, swapped or mixed with those of another. Admiral Sir Richard Knighton, Britain's Chief of the Defence Staff, said the London-led force would move from memorandums and meetings to practical measures between navies, air forces and land forces. The United States is not part of the joint naval forces. Canadian participation, by contrast, is under consideration.

In July 2026, El País reported that Washington had informed European allies that in a crisis it would cut the fighter aircraft it makes available to NATO by a third and halve its strategic bombers. It would also no longer provide submarines. By then, the United States had also withdrawn 5,000 troops from Germany, including an armored combat brigade and a long-range fires battalion. It had likewise suspended plans to station Tomahawk missiles on German territory.

The two initiatives show, Tore O. Sandvik, Norway's defence minister, said, that Europe and Canada are taking greater responsibility for maritime security and promoting more equitable burden-sharing in NATO. The result, he said, will be a stronger Europe and a stronger NATO. Next week HMS Prince of Wales will host the Atlantic Future Forum and the International Sea Power Conference in Oslo. Jenkins will lead the Royal Navy's involvement, with European military and political leaders attending.
