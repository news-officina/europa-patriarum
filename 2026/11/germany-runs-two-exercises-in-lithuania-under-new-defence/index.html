Germany runs two exercises in Lithuania under new defence declaration

More than 6,000 soldiers of the 10th Panzer Division take part from 6 November to 3 December, days after Kaunas and Pistorius signed a cooperation pact in Munich on 4 November.

6 November 2026

Days after Lithuania and Germany signed a strategic defence cooperation declaration, Germany's 10th Panzer Division is launching two exercises in Lithuania involving more than 6,000 of its soldiers. Freedom Shield 26-II and Grand Eagle 26 begin on 6 November 2026 and last until 3 December, coordinated by the division. Lithuanian Defence Minister Robertas Kaunas and German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius signed the declaration on 4 November, which the Lithuanian ministry places at the Munich Security Conference.

The two exercises serve the cooperation framework the ministers signed, under which Lithuania and Germany plan joint work on capability development and defence industry partnerships. According to the Lithuanian ministry, future acquisitions are to favour interoperable or interchangeable solutions, with EU financing in mind. The declaration also carries the German brigade concept, planned at roughly 5,000 personnel in Lithuania by the end of 2027, a figure the ministry gave in October.

On 17 November, Panzerbrigade 45 “Litauen”, with about 3,800 soldiers, opened Freedom Shield 26-II, its second such exercise of 2026, for ten days. The organisers describe it as the largest Bundeswehr field training exercise held in Lithuania so far. A central element is a water crossing over the Nemunas River south of Kaunas. For the first time, new loitering munition systems are to be tested during the exercise. It concludes with a six-day field training phase at Rudninkai.

In parallel, Panzergrenadierbrigade 37 "Freistaat Sachsen" is deploying to Lithuania for Grand Eagle 26. The brigade is available to NATO as a rapid reaction one until Panzerbrigade 45 "Litauen" is fully established, planned for the end of 2027. The 10th Panzer Division directs the brigades from its home base in Veitshöchheim, Bavaria. That divisional control runs from the alerting of the units until all of them have returned to their home stations.

The timetable for the rest of the stationing remains a draft in the Lithuanian ministry's plan. It sets the final details for a November Planning Conference, with the bulk movement of the brigade in the second half of 2027. The ministry's October figures put about 2,000 German military and civilian personnel in the country by the end of 2026, up from about 1,700 in September. The draft plan ends with the brigade fully operationally capable and completely stationed in Lithuania by the end of 2027.
