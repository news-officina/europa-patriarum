Eight dead and two missing off Crete as minister plans EU return hub initiative

The Greek coastguard rescued 28 survivors south of Kali Limenes, where three operations were coordinated by the JRCC with Frontex aircraft. Deputy Migration Minister Sevi Voloudaki says the migration minister will present an initiative with other member states within days.

9 November 2026

Eight migrants died and two remain missing after their boat was found off southern Crete, where the Greek coastguard rescued 28 survivors. Survivors said two more had fallen into the sea. Deputy Minister of Migration and Asylum Sevi Voloudaki said Migration Minister Pleuris would announce, within days, an initiative with other EU member states to create return hubs in a country outside Europe.

Control of search and rescue south of Crete rests with Greece's Joint Rescue Coordination Centre. This JRCC coordinated a second operation in which a Frontex aircraft spotted a boat carrying about 47 migrants some 30 nautical miles south of Kali Limenes. Frontex said about 31,800 crossings were recorded in the first three quarters of 2026, a fall of 15 percent compared with the same period last year.

On the afternoon of Friday, 7 November, the coast guard was informed of a dinghy in distress six nautical miles south-southwest of Kali Limenes. In a third search and rescue operation south of Crete, a Frontex unmanned aerial vehicle detected another boat carrying approximately 40 migrants. Frontex rescue teams picked up the survivors and brought them to Kali Limenes. One of four survivors taken to hospital after the boat incident later died.

The Heraklion Central Coast Guard Authority has opened a case file against two Sudanese nationals, aged 28 and 16, whom survivors identified as the smugglers. They face charges of illegal entry, facilitating illegal entry, forming a gang and fatal exposure. According to testimonies reported by Greek media, the migrants had set out from Libya on 3 or 4 November 2026. Survivors said they had paid 12,000 to 16,000 Libyan dinars each for the crossing to Greece.

Voloudaki put the toll of the recent sinkings at at least 20 migrants found dead in Crete, describing them as victims of smuggling networks. She said the cooperation that existed in the past with eastern Libya no longer operates, and that its loss has driven the flows up. Voloudaki warned that if the effort does not succeed within a short time, much stricter measures will be taken. These, she said, would be either the ones used last summer in similar cases, which she said worked, or others.
