At least 20 dead in two boats off Crete as minister plans EU return hub initiative

The Greek coastguard rescued 46 survivors from two boats south of Kali Limenes, and Frontex teams picked up about 87 more on Monday. Deputy Migration Minister Sevi Voloudaki says the migration minister will present an initiative with other member states within days.

9 November 2026

Correction: An earlier version left out a second boat, found off Crete early on Monday 9 November with 12 dead and 18 survivors, and placed Monday's details with the boat found on Saturday.

Twelve migrants were found dead and 18 were rescued from a dinghy off southern Crete in the early hours of Monday, two days after eight others died on another boat, from which the Greek coastguard rescued 28 survivors and two people remain missing. Deputy Minister of Migration and Asylum Sevi Voloudaki said Migration Minister Pleuris would announce, within days, an initiative with other EU member states to create return hubs in a country outside Europe.

Control of search and rescue south of Crete rests with Greece's Joint Rescue Coordination Centre. On Monday the JRCC also coordinated an operation in which a Frontex aircraft spotted a boat carrying about 47 migrants some 30 nautical miles south of Kali Limenes. Frontex said about 31,800 crossings were recorded in the first three quarters of 2026, a fall of 15 percent compared with the same period last year.

The Saturday boat had first been reported on Friday afternoon, a dinghy in distress six nautical miles south-southwest of Kali Limenes. In a further operation on Monday, a Frontex unmanned aerial vehicle detected another boat carrying approximately 40 migrants. Frontex rescue teams picked them up and brought them to Kali Limenes. One of four survivors of the Monday dinghy who were taken to hospital later died.

The Heraklion Central Coast Guard Authority has opened a case file against two Sudanese nationals, aged 28 and 16, whom survivors identified as the smugglers. They face charges of illegal entry, facilitating illegal entry, forming a gang and fatal exposure. According to testimonies reported by Greek media, the migrants had set out from Libya on 3 or 4 November 2026. Survivors said they had paid 12,000 to 16,000 Libyan dinars each for the crossing to Greece.

Voloudaki spoke of at least 20 migrants found dead in Crete, describing them as victims of smuggling networks. She said the cooperation that existed in the past with eastern Libya no longer operates, and that its loss has driven the flows up. Voloudaki warned that if the effort does not succeed within a short time, much stricter measures will be taken. These, she said, would be either the ones used last summer in similar cases, which she said worked, or others.
