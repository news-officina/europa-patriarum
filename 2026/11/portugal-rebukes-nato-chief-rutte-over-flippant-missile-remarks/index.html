Portugal rebukes NATO chief Rutte over 'flippant' missile remarks

The foreign ministry in Lisbon objected to remarks suggesting the government downplays the Russian threat, as Spain stayed silent.

11 November 2026

Mark Rutte said he tells allies in Spain and Portugal who call the war far away that Russia's newest missiles reach Lisbon five minutes later than Warsaw. Portugal's foreign ministry, led by Paulo Rangel, criticized him on X. It said it regretted the flippancy of remarks accusing Portugal of downplaying the Russian threat.

A Eurobarometer poll taken this spring found that 92 percent of those surveyed in Portugal saw Russia's invasion of Ukraine as a security threat to the EU. The ministry wrote on X that Rutte has an obligation to know which countries and governments are firmly on Ukraine's side and know the Russian threat well. Portugal, it said, its government and its people have never been in any doubt about which side of history they stand on.

Rutte was voicing an argument that capitals in the north and east have pressed for months. The Netherlands, Germany, the Nordic countries and the Baltics have held that southern allies give Kyiv less financial and military help than they should. Their list of southerners ran beyond Spain and Portugal to France and Italy, who they said see the war as distant. In a Dutch television interview, Rutte said he had heard from two allies in Spain and Portugal. What happens in Russia, he reported them saying, affects mainly Poland.

In January, Portugal's Prime Minister, Luís Montenegro, said Portugal could anticipate even further its arrival at 2 per cent of GDP in defence. The foreign ministry has also reaffirmed its support for the defence of Ukraine. The EU average was 76 per cent.

NATO defended the remarks through its spokesperson, Allison Hart. She said it is clear that all Allies agree that Russia is a threat, and that the Secretary General often explains to public audiences, who may be less engaged on security issues, how close that threat is to every Ally. She pointed to the Ankara summit in July, where leaders had confirmed the same. Spain, which was named alongside Portugal, has so far stayed silent: the government of Pedro Sánchez had not publicly responded to his accusation at the time of writing.
