Karasin says no agreement exists for Europeans to join the Abu Dhabi talks

Karasin called French plans of attendance wishful thinking, while Kallas says any agreement the EU is not party to will not be implemented. Costa has put EU participation on the agenda of Friday's informal leaders' meeting in Dublin.

9 November 2026

No Russian agreement exists for European states, including France, to join the Abu Dhabi talks. Grigory Karasin, first deputy chairman of the Federation Council's international affairs committee, called Paris's stated plans "wishful thinking". Earlier, French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said "the Europeans will be well-represented, and France will be in Abu Dhabi". Ursula von der Leyen said the EU "will be present alongside our partners".

Paula Pinho, its chief spokesperson, said any European security framework is for the EU to decide, because it is a matter of its competence and of EU security. Kaja Kallas said: "Any agreement that we are not party of, we are not going to implement either." Kallas, the bloc's foreign policy chief, argued that sanctions, frozen assets and membership rest on EU decisions. Radosław Sikorski, Poland's foreign minister, backed Giorgia Meloni's warning against Western Europe dealing over the heads of the East.

Steve Witkoff, the United States special envoy, announced on 3 November new trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November. He said the discussions would cover post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian efforts and next steps toward peace. According to Politico, an EU official said European representatives would not be inside the room.

EU leaders have been at loggerheads over how Europe should be represented, according to Politico. European Council President António Costa put EU participation in the peace talks on the agenda of the informal leaders' meeting in Dublin, set for Friday. Donald Trump, for his part, said a very important meeting was taking place and that he believes the war will end soon. He said the only reason it will end is because of him.

Moscow, for its part, has repeatedly rejected Western-backed proposals for a full and unconditional ceasefire and continues to demand that Ukraine cede Donbas.
